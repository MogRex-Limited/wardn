Plain RBAC gives each user a role for the whole system. That breaks the moment your product has more than one customer: Ada is an admin, but only of *her* bank.

The fix is to attach every role to a place. wardn calls that place a **scope**.

> Ada is `org_admin` **at organisation:acme**. Bayo is `teller` **at branch:lagos**.

## Scopes form a tree

Most products already have a natural structure. You declare it, and roles flow **down** it:

```text
global                                     root  → platform_admin   (everything, everywhere)
 ├── organisation:acme                     ada   → org_admin        (all of Acme)
 │    └── region:south                     seun  → auditor          (every branch in the South)
 │         ├── branch:lagos                bayo  → teller           (Lagos only)
 │         └── branch:ibadan
 └── organisation:globex                   (none of the above reach here)
```

- A role held at a scope applies there **and everywhere below it**. Seun audits Lagos and Ibadan because both are in the South.
- It never applies **above** or **beside** it. Bayo's teller role says nothing about Ibadan.
- Separate tenants are separate branches of the tree, so **tenant isolation** comes from the structure itself, not from a `WHERE tenant_id = ?` that someone has to remember.

## Every product's tree is different

| Product | Its scope tree |
|---|---|
| Bank | organisation › region › branch |
| SaaS | workspace › project |
| School system | district › school › class |
| Simple internal tool | none: one global scope |

The tree is yours to define. wardn knows nothing about banks or schools: it only knows that a `branch` sits under a `region`.

## Why not one role per tenant?

Without scopes, teams encode the place in the role name: `acme_admin`, `globex_admin`, `lagos_teller`. It works for a while, then:

- Every new customer or branch means new roles.
- "Who are the admins of Acme?" becomes a string search.
- Nothing stops a check from accepting `globex_admin` on Acme's data, because the computer doesn't know the names mean anything.

With scopes there is one `org_admin` role, and the question becomes *who holds `org_admin` at `organisation:acme`?*

## Check the resource's scope

Scopes protect data only if every check names the right one. A request to `/branches/ibadan/loans/7` passes a check for Ibadan, but loan 7 might belong to Lagos. This is the classic *confused deputy* bug, also called IDOR. The rule is to check against the scope **the resource belongs to**, or to look the resource up only within the scope you checked.

<Aside type="caution">
This is the single most common authorization bug in multi-tenant products. The [multi-tenancy guide](/guides/multi-tenancy/) shows the safe pattern: authorize first, then look up within that scope.
</Aside>
