**wardn is simple, opinionated authorization for Go.** It answers one question for any product: *is this user allowed to do this, here?*

Every product needs roles and permissions, and every product is different. A bank has organisations, regions and branches. A SaaS product has workspaces. A school has schools and classes. wardn doesn't pick a model for you. Each product describes its own world in one file, and wardn does everything else.

## What wardn does

- **Checks access** on every request, with a reason for every decision.
- **Protects HTTP routes** with one line of middleware, for `net/http` and chi.
- **Models tenancy and hierarchy**, so an organisation admin automatically acts in every branch of that organisation and nowhere else.
- **Stores who has which role** in your existing PostgreSQL, MySQL, MariaDB, SQLite or MongoDB database, and creates its tables for you.
- **Lets administrators create custom roles** at runtime, with an *escalation guard*: nobody can grant more power than they hold.
- **Grants access for a limited time**, such as a temporary approver until Friday, with no cleanup job required.
- **Records every change** before it happens, for your audit trail.

## What wardn does not do

wardn is deliberately focused. It does **not**:

- **authenticate users.** Your existing login, JWT or session code says *who* the user is; wardn decides *what they may do*.
- **evaluate business conditions** such as "approve loans under ₦5M". Check those in your code after wardn says yes.
- **model relationship graphs at Google scale.** If your core need is "editor of a folder can edit every document inside it" across millions of objects, use OpenFGA or SpiceDB.
- **run as a separate server.** wardn is a library inside your application.

## Opinionated, on purpose

wardn makes a small number of firm choices, so every product behaves the same way and developers learn it once:

| Choice | Why |
|---|---|
| **Default deny.** No role, no access. | Forgotten grants fail safe. |
| **Deny always wins** over any allow. | Restrictions can't be overridden by accident. |
| **Any error means "no".** | A database outage never opens the doors. |
| **Roles flow down the scope tree**, never up or sideways. | Tenants stay isolated. |
| **Permissions are code; roles are data.** | Admins can combine permissions, but never invent ones your code doesn't check. |
| **Typos stop the app at startup.** | Mistakes surface in development, not in production. |

None of these can be turned off. That's the point.
