wardn has four concepts. Everything else is built from them.

| Concept | What it is | Example | Defined by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permission** | An action your code checks | `loan:approve` | Developers, in `wardn.yaml` |
| **Role** | A named set of permissions | `teller` | Developers (system roles) or administrators (custom roles) |
| **Scope** | Where a role applies | `branch:lagos` | Your application's data |
| **Assignment** | A user holding a role in a scope | `bayo` is `teller` at `branch:lagos` | Administrators, at runtime |

A check asks: *does this user hold this permission at this scope?* wardn gathers the user's assignments at that scope and at every scope above it, and applies a few fixed rules.

```text
global
 └── organisation:acme                ada  → org_admin
      └── region:south
           └── branch:lagos           bayo → teller

Can bayo   do loan:create  at branch:lagos ?  yes (teller)
Can bayo   do loan:approve at branch:lagos ?  no  (no role grants it)
Can ada    do loan:approve at branch:lagos ?  yes (org_admin, inherited from acme)
Can ada    do loan:approve at organisation:globex ?  no (another tenant)
```
